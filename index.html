Objections, handled

The questions that actually stop deals.

Isn’t this just a spreadsheet with buttons?

A spreadsheet is a blank page. COUNTER already knows your glass thicknesses, your waste factor, your temper minimum, your edgework rate, your hardware and your margin — and it only asks the questions a customer can actually answer. The difference is that after one call, it takes thirty seconds instead of thirty minutes. It also has one thing a spreadsheet structurally can’t do: a 3D record of the job and a signature.

How is this different from the software I already pay for?

Mostly in what it doesn’t do. You probably already have more software than you use. COUNTER doesn’t replace your accounting, your scheduling, or your CAD — it owns exactly one thing: turning a conversation into a priced, signed quote fast. If your current tool already does that in thirty seconds, you don’t need me, and I’ll tell you that on the call.

What happens if I outgrow it, or I change my pricing?

Then you change the tool, and for the first ninety days that’s free. After that it’s a conversation. And because you hold the source code and the data, “outgrowing it” can never mean being trapped — you can have anyone maintain it, or maintain it yourself.

Will my estimators and techs actually use it?

That’s the only question that matters, and it’s why B6 exists. If they’re not using it, the build is wrong — not your team. So I hand over to the people doing the actual quoting, not to you, and I give you a fair test: live it, quote ten jobs, and if it hasn’t stuck I rebuild it free.

Do I own the code and my data?

Yes. All of it, exportable, in a format that isn’t a hostage situation. There is no per-seat licence and no monthly fee. If you walk away, you take everything and it still works. I’m confident enough in the build to want you to keep using it, not to need you to.

What do you need from me to start?

One call, and three real quotes — the last three jobs you priced, however messy, redacted if you need. That’s genuinely it. If your pricing lives in someone’s head rather than a document, that call is how we get it out, and the tool ends up more valuable for it.

I’m not a glass shop. Does this still apply?

Yes. Glass is where I start because the quoting pain is unusually sharp and the customer feedback is fast. But the mechanism — build the tool around the workflow, quote on the call, follow up inside ten minutes — is trade-agnostic. Window and door, fabrication, cabinetry, roofing, concrete, HVAC, landscaping, event hire. If you sell a custom thing that needs a number and a signature, this works.

What if I’m not ready to talk to you?

Then use the tools. The 3D quote builder at the top of this page is a real working example of the output, and the two calculators below it are genuinely free and genuinely yours. Come back when the pain is louder. The tool will still be here and I won’t have filled your inbox.
